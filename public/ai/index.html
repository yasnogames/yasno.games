Yasno Games uses AI

Primarily, we use Anthropic's product suite in our daily workflows. Workflows that make us:

  • Faster: writing code, debugging, updating documentation
  • More efficient: researching topics, gathering and analyzing data
  • And, we believe, achieving better outcomes

We're a (very) small husband and wife team, with one developer and gamer (that's me) and one boss in charge keeping things from going (too far) off the rails. So it's important we find a way to make progress without compromising our financial stability and long term goals.

And AI helps us do that.

But there are some things that we aren't going to use AI for as well:

  • Replace our vision: AI should never decide what outcomes are desired, that's up to us (and later our fans and customers as we begin to engage the community more directly)
  • Replace our voices: AI should never speak to you on our behalf
  • Replace our humanity: AI should never be a replacement for creative direction, artistic eye, or human connection

Will we make mistakes? Yes, in fact, we already have. In our prior devlogs we used Opus to draft the initial content and it was immediately evident. As a small team learning where and how AI speeds us up and where it would get in the way of our creativity, I'm sure we will make mistakes again. But we will listen, evolve, and thrive with the support and trust of the community.

What lines have we drawn so far?

  • We will not use AI generated assets. Our own assets are all procedural - currently including: the music, the sound effects, the models, the animations, the icons, the textures. We do use AI to write those procedural generators. (we also use and attribute some 3rd party assets such as sound banks)
  • We will not use AI to replace our own voice to the public, including devlogs, replies, or posts. We do use AI to polish and edit our own written voice and content.
  • We may accept suggested content from AI as-is without further edits (e.g. skill names or factual descriptions like help text). We do not accept such suggestions without critical review.

We wanted to be explicit about this, and we want to ensure our stance is clear to the public. We will endeavor to uphold these principles and where we fall short, we hope for two things of the public:

  • let us know when we make a mistake, holding us accountable to our promises, and
  • grant us understanding while we continue to navigate appropriate use of AI

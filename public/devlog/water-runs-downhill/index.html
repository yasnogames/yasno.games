Water runs downhill

24 Oct 2026 · Yasno Games

Our last post ended with "the rivers went in this month." That evening we deleted them: four layers of the world generator, 4,295 lines of code. The river above replaced them. It runs about five kilometres from where a new player starts.

The old rivers were routed between points 525 tiles apart. The generator knew the ground's height at those points and assumed a straight line between them, so wherever a ridge stood between two points, the river ran over it. In one stretch of the world we counted 20 places where a river climbed 16 metres or more. Every patch we made moved the climbs somewhere else.

The new generator builds the ground on hex cells of seven sizes, each three times finer than the one above, from cells 729 tiles across down to single tiles. Water is routed at every size. A river found among the big cells is handed down to the next size, which lays it over its own finer ground and finds smaller rivers there.

In its first days in the game the new water stood in the air, a staircase of blocks up the side of a mountain.

Then the rivers ran straight. Each size hands its ground down to the next as flat sloping triangles, and every stream on a flat slope takes the same steepest way down. A slope full of streams came out as a comb.

That is October 15, mid-afternoon.

What straightened out the comb was handing a river down whole. A level now passes each river's course to the next size, which bends it through the ground beside it, so a river picks up bends at every size. A stream that starts on a plain wanders toward whatever lies lower around it, so streams gather into trunks.

Those two are the same valley in our world viewer, which draws the generator's tiles without the game's look: on October 22, then today.

Lakes still go wrong. That one, yesterday morning, ends in a straight wall of water.

When they work, a stream runs down a wooded valley and fills a few ponds on the way.

The rivers come from a generator: seeded code, no trained model, nothing drawn by hand. Claude writes that code with us, and we decide what a river should look like and throw out what isn't one.
